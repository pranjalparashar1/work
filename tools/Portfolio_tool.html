Where the fund XIRR comes from, and what the funds cost

Two views of the same funds. The first shows how much of the equity XIRR rests on the top few funds; XIRR is recalculated from the transaction history each time a fund is left out. The second sets each fund's yearly fee against its gain over its benchmark,.

How to read this. Portfolio XIRR uses the dated purchases, redemptions and switches of the funds in view, plus today's value. Benchmark XIRR uses the same dated cash flows with the value the benchmark would have reached. Each fund and the group are worked out on the units held today, so they will not match the portfolio app, which includes units already sold. Rupee gain is current value minus the cost still held.

A fund's XIRR counts only money since it was first held in its current form. The older funds were held for years, and part of their gains was taken out through redemptions, so a small balance today can still carry a high XIRR. The funds bought in 2024 and 2025 have a short history, which makes their XIRR noisy. Where the benchmark would have been worth less than zero after the same redemptions (Aditya BSL Digital India, Tata Digital India, DSP ELSS), the file's own benchmark XIRR is shown for that fund. Past returns do not predict future returns.
